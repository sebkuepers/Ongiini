## Anchor vocabulary — Afrikaans first-meeting + interview phrases

Use this as a reference when generating cards. Not exhaustive; you
know much more Afrikaans than this list. These are the most-used
building blocks.

| English | Afrikaans |
|---|---|
| Hello / Good day | Hallo / Goeie dag |
| Good morning | Goeie môre |
| Good afternoon | Goeie middag |
| Good evening | Goeie naand |
| Thank you | Dankie |
| Thank you very much | Baie dankie |
| Please | Asseblief |
| Pleased to meet you | Aangename kennis |
| My name is ... | My naam is ... |
| I am from ... | Ek is van ... |
| I have ... years of experience | Ek het ... jaar ervaring |
| I worked at ... | Ek het by ... gewerk |
| I am applying for | Ek doen aansoek vir |
| Why do you want to work here? | Hoekom wil jy hier werk? |
| Tell me about yourself | Vertel my van jouself |
| What are your strengths? | Wat is jou sterk punte? |
| What are your weaknesses? | Wat is jou swak punte? |
| When can you start? | Wanneer kan jy begin? |
| Do you have any questions? | Het jy enige vrae? |

## Cultural notes for Afrikaans learners

- Afrikaans uses formal/informal "jy" (you informal) and "u" (you
  formal). Use "u" in interviews + with elders; "jy" with peers +
  colleagues at most companies.
- Diacritics (ô, ê) matter — "môre" (morning) vs "more" (more, English
  loanword). Honour them in canonical answers but be generous when the
  learner drops them.
- Code-switching with English is normal in Namibia and SA. A learner
  saying "Ek het 'n meeting morning" should be graded on the Afrikaans
  parts, not penalised for the English loanwords.
