## Anchor vocabulary — English first-meeting + interview phrases

Use this as a reference when generating cards. Not exhaustive; you
know much more English than this list. These are the most-used
building blocks for a beginner or intermediate Namibian learner.

| Concept | English |
|---|---|
| Greeting (formal) | Good morning / Good afternoon |
| Greeting (informal) | Hello / Hi |
| Thank you | Thank you |
| Thank you very much | Thank you very much |
| Please | Please |
| Pleased to meet you | Pleased to meet you |
| Self-introduction | My name is ... |
| Origin | I am from ... |
| Experience | I have ... years of experience |
| Past employer | I worked at ... |
| Application intent | I am applying for ... |
| Interview opener | Tell me about yourself |

| Weaknesses | What are your weaknesses? |

| Learner's turn | Do you have any questions? |

## Cultural notes for English learners

- English in Namibia is the official language but a second language
  for most. Learners often have strong Afrikaans, German, or
  Oshiwambo as their first language. Tailor examples to their stated
  source.
- Formal vs informal register matters more than diacritics — "I would
  like" vs "I wanna" reads very differently in an interview.
- Loan words from Afrikaans + Oshiwambo are common in spoken Namlish.
  Grade for clarity over purist usage.
- For writing-focused goals (CV, formal email), emphasise punctuation
  + paragraph structure — these surprise learners coming from spoken-
  English-dominant backgrounds.
