How these numbers are made

What the data is. Everything on this page is computed live from data Ongiini already keeps for the service itself — the per-message usage log, the structural trace log, and the long-term memory store. No raw conversation content leaves the foundation's infrastructure. The classification and clustering passes that produce the qualitative sections run on the same computer the chat service runs on; no third-party APIs are involved.

Two qualitative passes. The categories you see under How people use Ongiini.ai are not chosen from a fixed list. The local AI model first reads each user message and produces a short description — "yellowing maize leaves", "grade 11 chemistry homework", "VAT registration deadline". Then, periodically, the same model reads the entire collection of those descriptions with their counts and groups them into broad use cases of its own choosing. Top topics below is one level down — the most-mentioned raw descriptions, before clustering. Who uses Ongiini.ai works the same way per dimension: short descriptors per user → clustered into emergent groups for roles, regions, languages, family context, and current life situations.

This is a heavily skewed sample, not a survey. The percentages on this page describe only what users have mentioned in conversation. They are not a representative cross-section of who uses Ongiini.ai. A teacher who has never mentioned teaching does not appear under Roles → Teacher. A user in Oshakati who has never named their town does not show up under Regions → Oshakati. We do not ask, we only observe. Every WHO percentage is therefore relative to "users who mentioned this dimension at all" — usually a minority of the total user base — and the sample sizes shown next to each chart make that explicit. Read accordingly.

Sample size matters. Every chart shows how many observations underlie it. A cluster of "3 users" tells you something very different from a cluster of "3,000 users". When the service is small, the numbers are small. Treat anything with fewer than a few hundred observations as signal of direction, not measurement of magnitude.

Privacy floor. Any cluster or category represented by fewer than five people / fewer than five mentions is folded into "Other" so no single user can be identified.

Anti-leak guardrails on every label. The model is explicitly instructed to never echo names, towns, ages, dates, employers, or any identifying detail — and to output "REDACTED" if it cannot generalise a message without leaking specifics. Every label the model returns then passes a second, regex-level filter: anything containing a 4-digit number (years, IDs), a capitalised possessive ("Joseph's"), a known Namibian town name, or any pattern our PII scrubber recognises (email, ID number, card number, IBAN) is dropped without being stored. The label only enters the analytics cache once both layers have signed off.

Right to object. If you'd rather your data not be counted in any of these statistics — current or future — write to the address in our Privacy Policy with the subject "object to research processing" and your phone number. Your messages stop contributing immediately and retroactively.
